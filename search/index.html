Hashpower makes production possible and competing histories costly. Credible, coordinated rejection can reduce a profit-seeking attacker’s realizable payoff. That depends on actual enforcement, compatible settlement policies and continued accepted mining; one participant’s refusal cannot guarantee agreement.Content updated 2026-10-05 · Source scope inside
  2. Mechanism guide · Governance and responsibilityStewardship and authority in an open systemA shared system needs maintainers and coordination. Those responsibilities can be distributed across institutions with different scopes. The useful test is whether another competent participant can understand the rules, inspect a decision and continue the relevant work. BSV’s published Association powers remain real dependencies; plural stewardship is a standard to demonstrate, not evidence that concentration has disappeared.Content updated 2026-10-05 · Source scope inside

  7. Evidence brief · Trust & reputationHow should a serious person respond to the “scam” or “cult” label?Do not answer contempt with demands for loyalty. Translate the label into testable questions: What was promised? What works now? Who benefits? What is the user's risk? Which claims failed? Can the user leave? Evidence cannot prove that every participant is sincere, but a product can avoid manipulative patterns by making bounded claims, exposing dependencies, and offering a low-risk test path.Content updated 2026-10-05 · Source scope inside

  9. Evidence brief · Product fitDoes anything economically need BSV?Most software does not need a blockchain. Even then, the comparison is against ordinary databases, payment processors, and other ledgers on cost, latency, privacy, reliability, governance, and exit risk.Content updated 2026-10-05 · Source scope inside

Evidence brief · Consensus & securityCan nodes reject a longer proof-of-work chain and still claim Bitcoin rules?Block production, independent verification and economic acceptance are different jobs. A non-miner can check and refuse a result; it cannot produce replacement blocks or force everyone else to agree. Credible, coordinated rejection can reduce a profit-seeking attack’s payoff, conditional on enforcement, compatible settlement policies and continued accepted mining. The current BSV rules also assign institutional directive powers that need separate scrutiny.Content updated 2026-10-05 · Source scope inside
  14. Evidence brief · Asset recoveryCan BSV freeze or reassign coins?Yes, the Network Access Rules list directives that may freeze specified UTXOs, reassign frozen coins, alter peer access, or invalidate specified blocks after defined events. A reassignment enforced by nodes is not the same mechanism as forging the owner's signature or learning a private key, but it can change which spend the network accepts. That weakens pure bearer finality and creates legal-process and governance dependencies.Content updated 2026-10-05 · Source scope inside
  15. Evidence brief · GovernanceDoes the BSV Association function as a central authority?The Association is not merely an informal developer group. Its own materials describe protocol stewardship, and the NAR treat it as a party with rule and directive functions. Nodes still operate infrastructure and perform proof of work, but that does not erase the institutional layer. People who require authority-minimized bearer money may reject it; others may value explicit commercial rules. Both should evaluate the actual documents.Content updated 2026-10-05 · Source scope inside

  24. Evidence brief · SecurityWhat do hashpower and reorganization history mean for BSV security?Hashpower affects the cost of competing histories and remains essential to continuing production. Its success depends on actual enforcement and compatible policies, with sabotage and coordination failure still possible. Inclusion proofs do not establish absolute finality or current unspent status.Content updated 2026-10-05 · Source scope inside
  25. Evidence brief · Ecosystem accountabilityWhy have so many funded BSV projects disappeared?No one should defend every funded project.   26. Evidence brief · Design tradeoffsIs BSV just a cheap database for spam?BSV permits a broader use of transaction outputs and script than a money-only design. For workflows needing public timestamping, payment-linked events, or verifiable commitments, cheap blockspace can be useful. It is usually a mistake to publish private or bulky application data merely because fees are low. Good designs put only the minimum verification material on-chain and price the long-term externalities.Content updated 2026-10-05 · Source scope inside
  27. Evidence brief · Wallet trust modelCan miners, the chain, or an app sign as me or read my private data?Under the published wallet model, applications request operations from a wallet; the wallet holds or controls user key material and permissions. Miners validate transactions and order accepted history. The NAR can affect UTXO treatment at the network layer. None of those facts, by itself, gives a miner or Association the private keys needed to produce a user's application signature or decrypt properly protected data. Implementation bugs, wallet compromise, and poor permission design remain real risks.Content updated 2026-10-05 · Source scope inside

Evidence brief · Architecture & contingencyDoes Project Babbage depend too heavily on the BSV rail?Project Babbage builds a wallet-mediated application model around BRCs, keys, payments, certificates, and overlays currently anchored to BSV. Some concepts and application data can be portable; transaction history, satoshi-denominated payments, proof semantics, BSV-only code, and deployed services may not be. The responsible architecture is to label every boundary, minimize irreplaceable hosted dependencies, export user-controlled material, and maintain contingency triggers.Content updated 2026-10-05 · Source scope inside

  47. Glossary · GlossaryDARDigital Asset Recovery: the ecosystem term for processes intended to support freezing and reassignment of disputed or recoverable BSV through node-enforced rules.

  49. Glossary · GlossaryProof of workThe expenditure evidenced by finding a block hash below a target, used to make history costly to rewrite and to coordinate ordering among valid blocks.
  50. Glossary · GlossaryChainworkThe accumulated proof-of-work represented by a chain of block headers.

  53. Glossary · GlossaryBasketA wallet-level organization mechanism for outputs associated with an application or purpose.

  56. Glossary · GlossaryCounterpartyThe other party in a wallet key-derivation, payment, signature, encryption, or authenticated-message relationship.
