Can BSV freeze or reassign coins?

“BSV has an authority that can freeze coins, reassign property, or invalidate blocks. That is centralized control.”
Short answer

The answer without the theater.

BSV's Network Access Rules explicitly describe directives that can require nodes to freeze specified UTXOs, reassign frozen coins, blacklist peers, or invalidate blocks after defined events. That is a consequential governance choice. It is not the same mechanism as an app forging a signature or decrypting private data, but it does weaken a pure bearer-finality model.

What is true

The objection's strongest ground

  • The Network Access Rules form a legal agreement between the BSV Association and participating nodes under the terms stated in the rules.
  • Part III expressly lists freezing UTXOs, reassigning frozen coins, changing peer access, and invalidating specified blocks among possible directive steps.
  • People who want a ledger that can never reflect a legal recovery process may reasonably reject this model.
What remains unresolved

Do not claim more than the evidence

  • Public rules do not make courts infallible, guarantee narrow use, or eliminate concentration and process risk.
  • The existence of network-level recovery powers does not give ordinary applications a user's private keys, signatures, identity credentials, or encrypted data.
  • This page does not offer a legal conclusion about enforceability or asset classification.
Inspect it yourself

A better next move than arguing

  1. Read Part III, especially the directive events, restrictions, and permitted steps.
  2. Separate three layers in any threat model: wallet authority, application authority, and network/node governance.
  3. Ask for a public, complete history of directives, safeguards, appeals, and key-holder accountability.
Falsifiability

What would change this answer?

Evidence of secret directives, unbounded use, unavailable appeal, or materially broader powers than the published rules would worsen this assessment. Strong, independently auditable safeguards would improve it.
