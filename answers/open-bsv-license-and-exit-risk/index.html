Is the Open BSV License actually open—and can builders exit?

“Important BSV software is usable only on BSV. That creates legal and technical lock-in.”
Short answer

The answer without the theater.

The Open BSV License makes source available but contains a BSV-use condition. That is materially different from permissive licenses such as MIT or Apache-2.0 and creates exit risk for software that depends on it. The correct response is not to rename the risk away; it is to separate specifications, application code, libraries, hosted services, and rail-specific components and record the license of each.

What is true

The objection's strongest ground

  • Source availability and unrestricted cross-rail reuse are not the same property.
  • Several important BSV repositories identify the Open BSV License, while other ecosystem components use different licenses.
  • A builder's own application may be portable while a node, SDK dependency, or production service remains rail-bound.
What remains unresolved

Do not claim more than the evidence

  • This is a dependency assessment, not legal advice or a definitive interpretation of any license.
  • A public standard does not automatically guarantee multiple independent implementations or practical migration.
Inspect it yourself

A better next move than arguing

  1. Generate a dependency bill of materials and record every license before production adoption.
  2. Identify which interfaces can be reimplemented, which data can be exported, and which services are replaceable.
  3. Require counsel review when a rail-specific license is material to the product's exit plan.
Falsifiability

What would change this answer?

Clearer permissive licensing, independent implementations, and demonstrated migration paths would reduce the risk. More critical rail-specific dependencies would increase it.
