What do hashpower and reorganization history mean for BSV security?

“BSV has less hashpower than BTC and a reorg history. Governance responses cannot replace Nakamoto consensus.”

Answer with conditions · Updated 2026-10-05 · Next review 2026-11-05
By BSV.place / Project Babbage. Editorial review; source access dates below. No independent product retest is implied.

Short answer

The short answer

Hashpower affects the cost of competing histories and remains essential to continuing production. Economic acceptance also matters: credible, coordinated rejection can deny a profit-seeking attacker’s intended payoff. Its success depends on actual enforcement and compatible policies, with sabotage and coordination failure still possible. Inclusion proofs do not establish absolute finality or current unspent status.

Mechanism and practical meaning

Deterrence depends on realizable payoff and effective response

A profit-seeking attack needs counterparties to recognize a result from which its sponsor benefits. Effective rejection can remove that payoff in the target economy. That makes acceptance and coordination relevant to the economic model, alongside hashpower and the cost of competing histories.

This is conditional deterrence. It does not measure attack frequency, establish holdings from a pool label, or remove sabotage, liveness and concentration risks. Observed pool attribution, beneficial control and net economic exposure are separate facts. An incident record should say what was actually observed and which effects remain unknown.

What is true

The objection's strongest ground

  • Proof-of-work security is economic and relative, not a permanent property conferred by a chain name.
  • A valid-chain dispute, invalid transaction, double spend, accidental fork, and policy intervention are different events.
  • Confirmation policy should depend on value, reversibility, observed network conditions, and threat model.
What remains unresolved

Do not claim more than the evidence

  • No static confirmation number is safe for every value and failure mode.
  • Calling adversarial behavior dishonest does not remove the need to detect, price, and respond to it.
  • This site does not currently publish an independently audited incident database.
Inspect it yourself

What to inspect next

  1. Monitor chainwork, producer distribution, competing tips, stale blocks, double-spend signals, and the services relied upon.
  2. Document action thresholds for low, medium, and high-value workflows before accepting irreversible consequences.
  3. Reconstruct incidents from block data, node logs, alerts, and signed decisions rather than retrospective labels.
Open to better evidence

What would change this answer?

Material shifts in hash economics, producer diversity, attack history, monitoring, or rule enforcement should change the assessment quickly.
