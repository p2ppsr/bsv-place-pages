Does the BSV case depend on Craig Wright being Satoshi?

“BSV is Craig Wright. The court settled that question, so the rest is tainted.”
Short answer

The answer without the theater.

No. The High Court judgment in COPA v Wright is part of the historical record. A durable technical case for BSV cannot depend on reversing that judgment or asking a builder to adopt a personal identity claim. Evaluate protocol behavior, governance, economics, code, and actual use directly.

What is true

The objection's strongest ground

  • The High Court issued its approved judgment in COPA v Wright on May 20, 2024. Ignoring it makes a technical argument less credible, not more.
  • BSV's public reputation was shaped by years of Craig Wright and Satoshi-identity conflict. Builders inherit that reputational cost even when their software makes no identity claim.
  • A protocol, wallet interface, or application can be evaluated independently of the biography or claims of people historically associated with it.
What remains unresolved

Do not claim more than the evidence

  • Separating the software from Craig Wright does not erase the ecosystem's history or the institutional decisions made during it.
  • A runnable demo proves a capability, not broad adoption, sound governance, or commercial success.
Inspect it yourself

A better next move than arguing

  1. Read the judgment rather than social-media summaries of it.
  2. Run a BRC-100 quickstart and inspect the permissions requested by the application.
  3. Review the network rules and license terms as separate dependencies instead of treating a personality dispute as a substitute for technical diligence.
Falsifiability

What would change this answer?

If the practical stack required belief in an identity claim to function, obtain support, or exercise user rights, the separation argued here would fail.
