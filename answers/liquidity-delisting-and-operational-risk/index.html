Do delistings and weak liquidity make BSV unusable?

“BSV is hard to buy, sell, or hedge. Delistings and thin markets make it commercially irrelevant.”
Short answer

The answer without the theater.

Exchange access, fiat ramps, depth, spreads, custody support, and jurisdictional availability are real product risks. They should not be dismissed as price criticism. But their importance depends on the workflow. A product that continuously converts large balances has a different exposure from one that uses tiny transaction fees and keeps minimal treasury inventory.

What is true

The objection's strongest ground

  • A listed market can still have poor depth, wide spreads, withdrawal constraints, or jurisdictional exclusions.
  • Historic delisting lists become stale quickly; current access must be tested, not repeated from memory.
  • Price volatility and liquidity are not the same metric, though they can compound each other.
What remains unresolved

Do not claim more than the evidence

  • This site does not maintain an investment recommendation or promise that a particular exchange or ramp will remain available.
  • Low transaction fees do not solve treasury, accounting, tax, custody, or conversion risk.
Inspect it yourself

A better next move than arguing

  1. Test deposits, withdrawals, settlement time, spread, depth, limits, and legal availability in the actual operating jurisdiction.
  2. Minimize rail inventory when the product does not need market exposure.
  3. Write a fallback plan for provider loss before launch.
Falsifiability

What would change this answer?

Broader durable access and deeper markets would reduce this risk. Provider exits, shallow books, or unreliable withdrawals would increase it.
