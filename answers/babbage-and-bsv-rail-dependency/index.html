Does Project Babbage depend too heavily on the BSV rail?

“If BSV fails technically, economically, or politically, the wallet and app layer fails with it.”
Short answer

The answer without the theater.

Project Babbage builds a wallet-mediated application model around BRCs, keys, payments, certificates, and overlays currently anchored to BSV. Some concepts and application data can be portable; transaction history, satoshi-denominated payments, proof semantics, BSV-only code, and deployed services may not be. The responsible architecture is to label every boundary, minimize irreplaceable hosted dependencies, export user-controlled material, and maintain contingency triggers.

What is true

The objection's strongest ground

  • Current BRC-100 wallet flows and BSV transaction proofs are designed around the BSV ecosystem and settlement rail.
  • Public specifications and reusable application concepts can lower reimplementation cost without making migration automatic.
  • A rail contingency must cover funding, transaction semantics, proofs, identity references, overlays, hosted services, licenses, and user communication.
What remains unresolved

Do not claim more than the evidence

  • This site does not claim an existing drop-in replacement rail for the complete Babbage stack.
  • Exportable keys or records do not guarantee equivalent functionality, counterparties, or legal meaning elsewhere.
Inspect it yourself

A better next move than arguing

  1. Classify every component as rail-neutral, reimplementable, BSV-specific, hosted, or institution-controlled.
  2. Export user-controlled keys, certificates, records, baskets, and application data into documented formats.
  3. Run table-top failures for loss of a wallet provider, overlay host, broadcaster, exchange, node rule, and the rail itself.
Open to better evidence

What would change this answer?

Demonstrated replacement of critical services and portable implementations would reduce dependency. New BSV-only code, single-host services, or unavailable exports would increase it.
