Scale & economicsSourced answer

Is BSV scaling real, or a datacenter marketing claim?

“Teranode throughput is vendor hype. Large blocks centralize operators, and cheap fees reflect missing demand.”

Answer with conditions · Updated 2026-10-05 · Next review 2026-11-05
By BSV.place / Project Babbage. Editorial review; source access dates below. No independent product retest is implied.

Short answer

The short answer

BSV deliberately moves heavy validation and storage toward specialized block producers while expecting most applications to use headers, Merkle proofs, and services. Teranode source and a public testnet runner now make implementation more inspectable. A vendor or testnet benchmark still does not establish sustained mainnet throughput, economically useful demand, competitive block production, or reproducibility on disclosed hardware.

Mechanism and practical meaning

Specialization and lightweight verification have distinct benefits

Specialized block production can move heavy work away from ordinary application users, while relevant proofs let clients inspect what they need. This architecture is useful if service cost, availability and verification remain acceptable for the task. Its existence does not establish competitive production or economical demand.

Keep code, measured capacity, production use, operator diversity and total cost as separate records. A promotional daily transaction target is neither an independently reproduced benchmark nor evidence of customers. No new throughput test or current beneficial-control estimate was produced for this revision.

What is true

The objection's strongest ground

  • Larger blocks increase bandwidth, propagation, validation, and archival requirements somewhere in the system.
  • SPV reduces the verification burden for many clients; it does not make block production free or guarantee competitive mining.
  • A large historical block, a peak benchmark, a sustained production rate, low fees, and useful demand are different measurements.
What remains unresolved

Do not claim more than the evidence

  • This site does not treat Association-published throughput as independent reproduction.
  • Public source does not by itself establish production readiness, fault tolerance, or total operating cost.
Inspect it yourself

What to inspect next

  1. Record code revision, topology, hardware, workload, transaction mix, duration, latency, error rate, cost, and failure recovery for every benchmark.
  2. Compare current mainnet blocks, fee distribution, transaction mix, stale blocks, and producer distribution.
  3. Reproduce the test outside the maintainer's environment and publish raw results, including failures.
Open to better evidence

What would change this answer?

Independent sustained tests, transparent cost curves, production deployment, and broader operators would strengthen the claim. Unreproducible benchmarks or worsening concentration would weaken it.
